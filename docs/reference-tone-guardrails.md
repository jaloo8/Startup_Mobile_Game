# Reference Tone Guardrails

Use this guide when writing parody startup content (events, headlines, rivals, scenario flavor text).

## Purpose

Keep content relatable and funny while staying legally safe, non-defamatory, and broadly accessible.

## Rules

1. **Parody the archetype, not a real person**
   - Prefer patterns like "hype founder", "celebrity VC", "platform giant".
   - Avoid naming or clearly identifying real living individuals.

2. **No copyrighted dialogue or scene recreation**
   - Do not reuse famous lines from films/shows/books.
   - Write new wording that captures tone without copying phrasing.

3. **Keep conflicts non-graphic and neutral**
   - World events can reference market shocks, supply pressure, or policy shifts.
   - Avoid graphic war language, tragedy details, or partisan framing.

4. **Avoid direct defamation**
   - Do not present factual criminal/moral allegations about real entities.
   - If satire is needed, use fictionalized names and generic behaviors.

5. **Ground effects in gameplay clarity**
   - Every event should map to clear levers (growth, churn, revenue, cost, fundraising).
   - Write short descriptions that hint at why those levers move.

6. **Balance optimism and pressure**
   - Mix tailwinds and headwinds so the tone is not relentlessly negative.
   - Keep consequences meaningful but not catastrophic by default.

## Style Targets

- Voice: concise, dry startup humor, boardroom/newsroom tone.
- Length: 1-2 sentence descriptions, 3-10 word titles.
- Readability: avoid jargon chains unless the event itself is about jargon satire.

## Quick Pre-Ship Checklist

- [ ] No real-person names or identifiable quotes
- [ ] No partisan or graphic conflict framing
- [ ] Effect keys are valid and numerically balanced
- [ ] Title/description clearly explain why the effect happens
- [ ] Content still makes sense for non-technical players
